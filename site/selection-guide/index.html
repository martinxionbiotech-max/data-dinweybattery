    Truck Battery Selection Guide¶

    TL;DR — Choose a truck battery in four steps: (1) confirm the group size, (2) check the cold cranking amp (CCA) requirement, (3) verify the electrical system voltage (12V or 24V), and (4) account for climate. Getting the group size and CCA wrong is the #1 cause of fleet breakdowns.

    Step 1: Confirm the Group Size¶

    The group size is the physical footprint and terminal layout of the battery. It must match your vehicle's battery tray and cable reach.

    Standard Common truck group sizes Market
    BCI Group 31, 8D, 4D North America
    DIN / EN H8 (L5), H9 (L6) Europe
    JIS Large commercial codes Japan / Asia

    Always read the group size printed on your existing battery, or consult the vehicle manual.

    Step 2: Check the CCA Requirement¶

    Cold cranking amps (CCA) measures a battery's ability to start an engine in cold temperatures. Diesel engines need more CCA than gasoline engines of the same size.

    Truck type Typical CCA range (guidance only)
    Light truck / pickup 600–800 CCA
    Medium-duty truck 800–1000 CCA
    Heavy-duty / semi 850–1100+ CCA

    Confirm against your manual

    These are general ranges. The exact CCA requirement is printed in your vehicle manual and on the original battery label.

    Step 3: Verify the System Voltage¶

    • 12V — light trucks and pickups
    • 24V — heavy trucks, semi trailers and buses (two 12V batteries in series)

    Ordering the wrong voltage is a costly mistake. See the 12V vs 24V guide.

    Step 4: Account for Climate¶

    Colder climates require higher CCA. In extreme cold, consider AGM batteries, which perform better at low temperatures than flooded lead-acid.
