# Fleet Battery Management Guide

**TL;DR** — The most expensive truck battery is the one that fails on the road. Fleet
operators cut costs by standardizing battery specs, testing proactively (at least annually),
tracking age, and replacing on a schedule — not waiting for a breakdown.

## Why Battery Management Matters

A roadside battery failure costs far more than the battery: towing, downtime, missed
deliveries and driver idle time. A systematic approach turns batteries from a random failure
point into a predictable, budgeted maintenance item.

## 1. Standardize Your Specs

- Pick **one or two group sizes** and CCA ratings that cover your fleet
- This reduces inventory, simplifies procurement and prevents mis-specification
- Match the standard to your market (JIS for Asia/Africa/Middle East, DIN for Europe, BCI for
  North America)

## 2. Test Proactively

| Test | Frequency | What it catches |
|---|---|---|
| Load test (CCA) | At least annually | Weak batteries before failure |
| Voltage check | Every service | Charging-system faults |
| Visual inspection | Every service | Corrosion, swelling, loose terminals |

Test more often in harsh climates and high-mileage fleets. Always test before winter.

## 3. Track Battery Age

Record install date, group size, CCA rating and test results per vehicle. Heavy-duty batteries
typically last 3–5 years; tracking age lets you replace proactively at the right time.

## 4. Replace on a Schedule

Replace batteries at the 3–5 year mark, or sooner if they fail a load test. A scheduled
replacement is always cheaper than an unscheduled breakdown.

## 5. Match Technology to Duty Cycle

- **Standard long-haul** — flooded is economical
- **High vibration / cold climate** — AGM justifies its premium
- **Start-stop / demanding** — EFB or AGM
