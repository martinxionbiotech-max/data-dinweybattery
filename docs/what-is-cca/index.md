# What Is CCA & How Much Does a Diesel Truck Need?

**TL;DR** — Cold Cranking Amps (CCA) measures a battery's cold-start power: the amps it can
deliver for 30 seconds at −18°C while staying above 7.2V. Diesel trucks need more CCA than
gasoline vehicles — typically 600–800 for light trucks, 800–1000 for medium-duty, and 850–1100+
for heavy semis. Match CCA to your engine and climate.

## What CCA Measures

CCA is the industry-standard way to compare cold-start performance. The test is defined by
standards bodies (SAE J537 in North America, EN 50342-1 in Europe, JIS D5301 in Japan):

1. Cool the battery to −18°C (0°F)
2. Discharge it at a specified current for 30 seconds
3. The battery must stay above 7.2V (for a 12V battery)

!!! note "Standard-to-standard temperature difference"
    SAE J537 and EN 50342-1 test at −18°C, but JIS D5301 tests at −15°C. Because JIS tests
    at a warmer temperature, a JIS CCA figure is not directly comparable to an SAE or EN
    figure. Always compare CCA within the same standard.

The higher the CCA, the more current the battery can deliver in cold conditions — which is
exactly when an engine is hardest to crank and the battery is weakest.

## Why Diesel Engines Need More CCA

Diesel engines use compression ignition, with compression ratios roughly double those of
gasoline engines. This means:

- The starter motor must turn the engine against much higher resistance
- Cold engine oil is thicker, adding more resistance
- The starter draws more current as a result

So a diesel truck needs a materially higher CCA rating than a gasoline vehicle of comparable
size.

## CCA Requirements by Truck Class

| Truck class | Typical CCA range (guidance only) |
|---|---|
| Light truck / pickup (gasoline) | 400–600 CCA |
| Light truck / pickup (diesel) | 600–800 CCA |
| Medium-duty truck | 800–1000 CCA |
| Heavy-duty / semi | 850–1100+ CCA |

!!! warning "These are ranges, not exact specs"
    Every engine and climate is different. The authoritative CCA requirement is printed in your
    vehicle manual and on the original battery label. Use this table for planning, then confirm.

## CCA vs Other Ratings

| Rating | What it measures | When it matters |
|---|---|---|
| CCA | Cold-start power at −18°C | Cold and temperate climates |
| CA | Cranking amps at 0°C | Warmer baseline (higher number than CCA) |
| RC | Reserve capacity in minutes | Emergency runtime without alternator |
| Ah (C20) | Total energy storage | Heavy accessory/electronics loads |

CCA and Ah measure different things. A battery can have high CCA but modest capacity, or vice
versa — do not confuse the two.

## How Climate Changes the Math

- **Cold climates** — CCA is the primary concern. Choose the highest CCA in your group size,
  and consider AGM for the most reliable cold starts.
- **Hot climates** — CCA is less critical. Focus on capacity (Ah) and heat tolerance, since
  heat accelerates battery aging.
