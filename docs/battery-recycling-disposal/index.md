# Truck Battery Recycling & Disposal

**TL;DR** — Lead-acid batteries are the most recycled product on Earth — a ~99% recovery rate,
with lead (~60% of a battery's weight), plastic and electrolyte all reused. Never throw a truck
battery in general waste — return it to a licensed recycler or collection point.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Lead-acid is the most recycled product on Earth** — nearly 100% of the battery is recoverable (lead, plastic, electrolyte), making recycling an economic loop, not just an obligation.
2. **A "dead" battery still has scrap value** — with lead at ~60% of its weight, a used battery is a box of recoverable metal, which is why many recyclers pay for it; disposal can be revenue, not a cost.
3. **Never put a lead-acid battery in general waste** — lead is toxic and sulfuric acid corrosive; with a ~99% recycling rate already in place, landfill disposal is both illegal in most countries and a waste of recoverable metal.
4. **Store before recycling with discipline** — upright, away from ignition, on an acid-safe surface with terminals protected; a pile of old batteries is a 60%-lead, acid-filled hazard, not a scrap pile.
5. **Economics, not sentiment, drive the ~99% recovery rate** — lead at ~60% of the weight, infinitely recyclable, with ~80% of new-battery lead coming from scrap; smelting pays, which is why collection networks exist without regulation forcing them.

## The World's Most Recycled Product

Lead-acid batteries are widely recognized as the most recycled consumer product globally.
Because the materials (lead, plastic, electrolyte) are all recoverable, the recycling loop is
highly efficient — a large portion of the lead in a new battery comes from recycled batteries.

### Why Lead-Acid Is So Recyclable

The reason is practical, not sentimental: the battery is heavy, toxic and — crucially —
*nearly 100% recoverable*. In the US the lead-acid recycling rate is ~99%, the highest of any
consumer product. Lead makes up roughly 60% of a battery's weight, and because lead is
infinitely recyclable with no loss of quality, about 80% of the lead in a new battery is
recycled lead. A lead-acid battery is essentially a box of lead plates in a plastic case filled
with acid, with no exotic materials to separate — which is why smelting pays and scrap value is
often paid for used batteries.

## What Gets Recovered

| Material | How it is recovered |
|---|---|
| Lead | Plates are melted and refined for reuse |
| Plastic | The case is reground into new cases |
| Electrolyte | Neutralized or reclaimed as sodium sulfate |

## Why Proper Disposal Matters

Lead is toxic, and sulfuric acid is corrosive. Improper disposal contaminates soil and water.
That is why most countries require batteries to be returned to a licensed recycler rather than
disposed of in general waste.

## How to Recycle a Truck Battery

1. **Return it** — most battery retailers and distributors accept old batteries
2. **Sell it** — many recyclers pay for used lead-acid batteries for their recoverable lead
3. **Use a licensed recycler** — confirm the facility handles lead-acid batteries properly

## Safe Storage Before Recycling

- Store upright, away from ignition sources
- Place on a surface that won't be damaged by acid
- Prevent terminal short circuits
- Recycle promptly — don't accumulate old batteries
