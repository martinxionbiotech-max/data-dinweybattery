# AGM vs Flooded for Heavy-Duty Trucks

**TL;DR** — AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) batteries outperform flooded lead-acid in vibration
resistance, cold-start reliability, cycle life and charge acceptance, but cost roughly
1.8–2.2× more. For demanding heavy-duty duty cycles, AGM often pays for itself; for standard
conventional use, flooded remains the economical choice.

## How They Differ

- **Flooded lead-acid** — liquid electrolyte with lead plates. Proven, low-cost, the workhorse
  of conventional vehicles.
- **AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat)** — the electrolyte is absorbed into a glass mat separator,
  making the battery sealed, spill-proof and vibration-resistant.

| Specification | SLI (Flooded) | AGM |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle life @ 25°C | 200–300 cycles | 600–1200 cycles |
| Charge acceptance | Standard (1.0×) | 2.5–3.5× SLI |
| Vibration resistance | Moderate | High |
| Maintenance | Low (Ca-Ca) | Maintenance-free |
| Cold-crank reliability | Good | Better |
| Relative cost | 1.0× baseline | 1.8–2.2× |

*Data from Chengguang Energy technical documentation.*

## When to Choose AGM

- **High-vibration applications** — off-highway, mining, construction
- **Cold / Arctic climates** — where reliable cold starts matter most
- **Start-stop vehicles** — AGM handles frequent restarts better
- **Premium fleets** — where downtime costs outweigh the battery price premium

## When Flooded Is Enough

- **Standard long-haul duty** with healthy charging systems
- **Hot climates** where capacity (Ah) matters more than CCA
- **Budget-sensitive fleets** replacing batteries on a regular schedule

## Cost vs Life: The Simple Math

AGM costs roughly twice a flooded battery but typically offers roughly double the cycle life
and better reliability. In applications where a flooded battery is stressed (vibration, cold,
frequent starts), AGM's longer service life can offset its higher price. In gentle duty cycles,
flooded remains the better value.
