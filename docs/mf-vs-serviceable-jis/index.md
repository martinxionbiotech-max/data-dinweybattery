# Maintenance-Free (MF) vs Serviceable JIS Truck Batteries

**TL;DR** — JIS heavy-duty batteries come in two designs: maintenance-free (MF, calcium-based,
sealed) and serviceable (antimony thick-plate, needs water top-up). MF trades some deep-cycle
robustness for zero-maintenance convenience; the traditional Pb-Sb design is often kept on
heavy trucks precisely because it survives hard duty better.

## The Two Designs

| Feature | MF / SMF (sealed) | Serviceable (Pb-Sb) |
|---|---|---|
| Grid alloy | Calcium (Ca-Ca) | Lead-antimony (Pb-Sb) |
| Water loss | Very low | Higher — needs top-up |
| Sealed | Yes | Removable caps |
| Deep-cycle tolerance | Moderate | High |
| Vibration tolerance | Good | High |
| Maintenance | None | Periodic distilled-water check |

## Why Calcium Reduces Water Loss

In a calcium-alloy battery, the calcium raises the charging voltage at which the electrolyte
breaks down into oxygen and hydrogen. Because the battery stays below that threshold in normal
charging, very little water is lost — which is what makes the battery "maintenance-free."

Antimony, by contrast, lowers that threshold, so antimony batteries "gas" more during charging
and lose water — requiring periodic top-up. That is the core trade-off: antimony tolerates deep
cycling and vibration better, but needs more maintenance.

## What This Means for Heavy Trucks

Heavy trucks subject batteries to deep cycling, vibration and heat. Many heavy-duty JIS
batteries therefore keep the **thick-plate Pb-Sb design** — not because it is low-tech, but
because it survives that duty better over a long service life.

MF/SMF batteries win on **convenience and total cost of maintenance**, which suits fleets that
want zero-touch batteries and have well-maintained charging systems.

## Choosing for Your Application

| Application | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Heavy truck, harsh duty, vibration | Thick-plate Pb-Sb (serviceable) |
| Fleet seeking zero maintenance | MF / SMF |
| Cold climate, demanding starts | AGM (sealed, high CCA) |
| Standard long-haul | Either — match to fleet preference |

## A Note on Market Terminology

In the JIS aftermarket — particularly across the Middle East, Africa and Southeast Asia — "N150"
and "N200" are commonly specified with an **SMF** (sealed maintenance-free) variant alongside
the traditional wet version. Buyers should confirm which variant they are quoting, because the
technology — and the price and performance — differ.
