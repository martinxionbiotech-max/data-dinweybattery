# What Is an EFB Battery (for Commercial Vehicles)?

**TL;DR** — EFB (Enhanced Flooded Battery) is a step up from standard flooded lead-acid: it
adds carbon-enhanced plates and a polyester scrim separator for better charge acceptance and
cycle life. It sits between flooded and AGM in both performance and price.

## What "Enhanced Flooded" Actually Means

A standard SLI battery is a flooded lead-acid cell: lead plates sit in a free-flowing liquid
electrolyte. EFB keeps that wet design but strengthens the two parts that fail first under
repeated partial-state-of-charge cycling:

- **Carbon-enhanced negative plates** — adding carbon to the negative plate suppresses
  sulfation, the hardening of lead sulfate crystals that gradually strangles a battery in
  stop-start and partial-charge service.
- **Polyester scrim separator** — a woven reinforcing layer on the plate surface holds the
  active material in place, resisting the shedding that vibration and frequent cycling cause.

These two changes deliver the practical gains below without moving to AGM's sealed, mat-based
construction — which is why EFB is usually described as the "mid-tier" option between SLI and AGM.

## EFB vs Standard Flooded vs AGM

| Specification | SLI (Flooded) | EFB | AGM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | Flooded lead-acid | Enhanced flooded | Absorbent Glass Mat |
| Cycle life @ 25°C | 200–300 | 400–600 | 600–1200 |
| Charge acceptance | 1.0× | 1.5–2.0× SLI | 2.5–3.5× SLI |
| Start-stop compatible | No | Yes (entry-level) | Yes (full) |
| Sealed / spill-proof | No | No | Yes |
| Relative cost | 1.0× | 1.3–1.5× | 1.8–2.2× |

*Cycle-life and charge-acceptance figures are typical ranges from Chengguang Energy technical
documentation — real values vary by model, duty cycle and climate.*

## Why Charge Acceptance Matters for Commercial Vehicles

Modern trucks carry far more electrical load than a basic starting battery was designed for —
telematics, refrigeration, hydraulic lifts, inverters, and increasingly stop-start or
micro-hybrid functions. "Charge acceptance" is how quickly the battery can absorb energy
returned by the alternator during a drive cycle.

A standard flooded battery accepts charge relatively slowly, so in a high-load vehicle it can
spend too much time under-charged, accelerating sulfation and early failure. EFB's roughly
1.5–2× charge acceptance keeps the battery closer to full charge between cycles — the single
biggest reason fleet operators see longer service life from EFB than from SLI in demanding
duty.

## When to Choose EFB

- **Entry-level start-stop systems** — where standard flooded is not durable enough
- **Mid-tier commercial vehicles** — needing better cycle life than flooded without AGM's cost
- **Higher electrical loads** — telematics, lifts and accessories that demand faster recharging
- **Vibration-heavy urban delivery** — the scrim separator resists plate shedding better than SLI

## When to Choose Something Else

- **Conventional, gentle duty** — standard flooded is sufficient and cheapest
- **Demanding, high-vibration, cold-climate** — AGM's superior cold-cranking and cycle life
  justify its cost
- **Deep-cycling applications** — a true deep-cycle or AGM battery is the right tool, not EFB

## Charging and Maintenance

EFB charges on the same voltage profile as standard flooded, so it rarely needs a dedicated
charger. Two practical notes:

- **Keep the charging system in spec** — an over-voltage alternator ages any lead-acid battery
  faster, EFB included.
- **Do not let it sit deeply discharged** — like all lead-acid chemistry, prolonged deep
  discharge permanently reduces capacity.
