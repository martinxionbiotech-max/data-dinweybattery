# Truck Battery Brands Compared

**TL;DR** — Compare truck battery brands on objective criteria — verified specs, certifications,
documentation and manufacturing consistency — not marketing claims or brand reputation alone.
A documented, certified manufacturer can match or beat a big brand at a better price.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Certifications, not logos, signal quality** — IATF 16949 is the automotive-specific standard; ISO 9001 is a general one, and the gap is the supply-chain discipline a fleet buyer needs.
2. **Batch consistency beats brand fame** — per-batch CCA test data proves the battery was tested before shipping; a famous logo only proves the marketing was good.
3. **ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 tell the hidden story** — environmental and occupational-health certs reveal the operational maturity a marketing sheet hides.
4. **Scale is a reliability proxy** — a 200,000 m² facility running 18 lines at 40,000 batteries daily implies repeatable output, not hand-built one-offs.
5. **An OEM factory can beat big-brand price** — with IATF 16949 and per-batch documentation you skip the brand premium without sacrificing consistency.

## The Criteria That Actually Matter

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Verified specs | Real CCA and capacity | Manufacturer data sheets, not marketing |
| Certifications | Quality assurance | IATF 16949, ISO 9001, ISO 14001, CE |
| Batch documentation | Consistency | Per-batch CCA test data, COA, MSDS |
| Manufacturing scale | Reliability | Established facility, consistent output |
| Warranty | Risk protection | Clear terms, reasonable period |

## Certifications to Look For

- **IATF 16949** — the automotive quality management standard; the strongest quality signal
- **ISO 9001:2015** — general quality management
- **ISO 14001:2015** — environmental management
- **CE marking** — European conformity

### Why IATF 16949 Is the Deciding Signal

IATF 16949 is specifically the automotive-sector quality standard — it is the same family of
requirements that automotive OEMs impose on their component suppliers. A battery manufacturer
holding IATF 16949 is running a production and traceability system built for the automotive
supply chain, which is exactly the discipline a fleet or distribution buyer should want. ISO
9001 is a strong general signal; IATF 16949 is the automotive-specific one.

## Why Batch Consistency Beats Brand Name

For fleet and distribution buyers, the critical question is: *will every battery perform the
same?* A manufacturer running an IATF 16949 quality system with per-batch test data provides
that consistency — regardless of how famous the brand is.

A famous brand tells you the marketing was good. Per-batch CCA test data tells you the
battery was actually tested before it shipped. When a fleet runs hundreds of batteries, that
difference compounds into fewer roadside failures and more predictable replacement schedules.

## Questions to Ask a Supplier

1. What certifications does your factory hold?
2. Can you provide per-batch CCA test data and a certificate of analysis?
3. What is your MOQ and lead time?
4. What warranty do you offer?
5. Do you support OEM / private label?
