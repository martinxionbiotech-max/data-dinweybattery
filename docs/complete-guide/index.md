faq:
  - q: What group size battery does my truck need?
    a: Match the group size printed on your existing battery or in the vehicle manual. Heavy-duty trucks in JIS markets commonly use 145G51 (N150) or 190H52 (N200); European trucks use DIN88 or DIN100; North American trucks use BCI Group 31 (available on request from DINWEY).
  - q: How much CCA does a diesel truck need?
    a: Light trucks and pickups typically need 600–800 CCA, medium-duty trucks 800–1000 CCA, and heavy-duty semi trucks 850–1100+ CCA. These are general ranges — confirm the exact requirement in your vehicle manual and on the original battery label.
  - q: Is my truck 12V or 24V?
    a: Light trucks and pickups use 12V. Heavy trucks, semi trailers and buses typically use 24V systems built from two 12V batteries wired in series. Check your battery compartment or manual.
  - q: What is the difference between CCA and Ah?
    a: CCA (cold cranking amps) measures cold-start power; Ah (amp-hours) measures total energy storage capacity. A battery can have high CCA and low Ah, or vice versa — they measure different things and are not interchangeable.
  - q: How long does a truck battery last?
    a: A heavy-duty truck battery typically lasts 3–5 years depending on climate, duty cycle, charging system health and vibration. Extreme cold or heat, deep discharges and poor charging shorten service life.
  - q: Can I mix two different batteries in a 24V system?
    a: No. In a 24V series system, replace both batteries as a matched pair of the same model, age and rating. Mismatched batteries charge and discharge unevenly, shortening service life.

# Truck Battery Complete Guide

![DINWEY 190H52 N200 heavy-duty truck battery](../assets/190H52-N200.jpg)

**TL;DR** — Choose a truck starting battery by matching four things: (1) the group size that
fits your tray, (2) the CCA your diesel engine needs, (3) the system voltage (12V or 24V), and
(4) your climate. In JIS markets the heavy-duty workhorses are N150/N200; in Europe they are
DIN88/DIN100; in North America BCI Group 31.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Match four specs, not one** — group size, CCA, voltage and climate all have to be right; the 190H52's 1100 A CCA is worthless if its 520×278×220 mm footprint won't fit the tray.
2. **Diesel CCA climbs with truck class** — 600–800 for light trucks, 800–1000 medium-duty, 850–1100+ for heavy semis; a gasoline-spec battery on a diesel is a winter-start trap.
3. **CCA and Ah measure different things** — the 145G51 (N150) holds 135 Ah with 900 A CCA while the 60038 (DIN100) stores only 100 Ah at 870 A EN; starting power ≠ stored energy.
4. **AGM costs 1.8–2.2× flooded but cycles 3–6× longer** — 600–1200 cycles vs 200–300 at 25°C; the premium pays back in harsh duty, not light duty.
5. **24V means two matched 12V batteries in series** — never mix models or ages, or the pair charges unevenly and falls short of the 3–5 year service norm.

## What a Truck Battery Does

A truck starting battery (also called an SLI battery — Starting, Lighting, Ignition) delivers a
short, high-current burst to crank a large diesel engine, then is recharged by the alternator.
Unlike a deep-cycle battery, it is not designed to be deeply discharged — it is built for high
cold cranking amps and fast recharge.

## Step 1: Match the Group Size

The group size is the physical footprint, terminal type and terminal orientation. It must match
your battery tray and cable reach.

| Standard | Market | Heavy-duty truck models | Key rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| JIS | Japan, Asia, Middle East, Africa | 145G51 (N150), 190H52 (N200) | CCA, RC, Ah |
| DIN / EN | Europe, Middle East, North Africa | 58827 (DIN88), 60038 (DIN100) | EN cold-cranking amps, Ah |
| BCI | North America, Latin America | Group 31, 8D, 4D | CCA (SAE J537), RC |

!!! note "Read the label"
    The group size is printed on your existing battery (for example "N150" or "DIN100") and in
    your vehicle manual. Never guess — a battery that does not fit or has the wrong terminal
    orientation cannot be safely installed.

## Step 2: Check the CCA Requirement

Cold cranking amps (CCA) is the current a battery can deliver for 30 seconds at −18°C (0°F)
while staying above 7.2V. Diesel engines need more CCA than gasoline engines because of their
higher compression.

| Truck type | Typical CCA range (guidance only) |
|---|---|
| Light truck / pickup | 600–800 CCA |
| Medium-duty truck | 800–1000 CCA |
| Heavy-duty / semi | 850–1100+ CCA |

These are general ranges. The exact requirement is printed in your manual and on the original
battery label — in cold climates, err on the higher side.

## Step 3: Verify the Voltage

- **12V** — light trucks, pickups and most light/medium commercial vehicles
- **24V** — heavy trucks, semi trailers, buses and some off-highway equipment

A 24V system is typically two 12V batteries wired in series. See our
[12V vs 24V guide](../12v-vs-24v/index.md) for the full picture.

## Step 4: Account for Climate

Cold reduces battery performance, so colder climates need higher CCA. In extreme cold, consider
AGM batteries, which deliver more reliable cold starts than flooded lead-acid. In hot climates,
CCA is less critical and capacity (Ah) plus heat tolerance matter more.

## Battery Technologies Compared

| Specification | SLI (Flooded) | EFB | AGM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycle life @ 25°C | 200–300 cycles | 400–600 cycles | 600–1200 cycles |
| Charge acceptance | Standard | 1.5–2.0× SLI | 2.5–3.5× SLI |
| Maintenance | Low (Ca-Ca) | Low | Maintenance-free |
| Start-stop compatible | No | Yes (entry) | Yes (full) |
| Relative cost | 1.0× baseline | 1.3–1.5× | 1.8–2.2× |
| Best use | Conventional vehicles | Mid-tier, entry start-stop | Premium, demanding duty |
