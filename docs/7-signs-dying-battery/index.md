# 7 Signs Your Truck Battery Is Dying

**TL;DR** — Watch for slow cranking, dim lights, frequent jump-starts, a swollen case, a
sulfur smell, an old battery (3–5+ years) and corrosion. Any of these means it is time to
load-test the battery — ideally before it strands you.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Slow cranking is the earliest, most reliable warning** — especially when cold, it means cranking power is already fading long before a full no-start.
2. **Act on the first sign, not the seventh** — a slow crank in September becomes a no-start in January; the decline is gradual but the failure is sudden.
3. **Age 3–5+ years is itself a warning sign** — once a battery is in that window and shows any other symptom, replace it proactively rather than gambling.
4. **A swollen case or sulfur smell is a safety issue** — both signal overcharging or overheating and venting gas; replace promptly and handle with care.
5. **More than one jump-start is the hard evidence** — needing a jump twice means the battery can no longer hold a charge, whatever a voltage reading says.

## 1. Slow or Labored Cranking

If the engine turns over more slowly than usual, especially when cold, the battery is losing
its cranking power. This is the most common early warning sign.

## 2. Dim or Flickering Lights

Headlights that dim at idle or flicker under load suggest the battery is not holding voltage —
or the charging system is failing.

## 3. Frequent Jump-Starts

Needing a jump-start more than once is a clear sign the battery can no longer hold a charge.

## 4. Swollen or Bulging Case

A bloated case means the battery has been overcharged or overheated. This is a safety risk —
replace it promptly.

## 5. Sulfur / Rotten-Egg Smell

A sulfur smell indicates the battery is overheating and venting gas, often from overcharging or
an internal fault. Handle with care.

## 6. Age (3–5+ Years)

Heavy-duty truck batteries typically last 3–5 years. If yours is in that window and showing any
other sign, replace it proactively.

## 7. Corroded or Loose Terminals

Corrosion adds resistance and loose terminals cause intermittent contact. Clean and tighten
them; if the battery still underperforms, it may be failing.

## What to Do Next

If you see one or more of these signs, load-test the battery. A professional load test measures
whether the battery still delivers its rated CCA. If it fails, replace it with the correct
group size and CCA for your truck.

The key point is to act on the *first* sign rather than waiting for several. A slow crank in
September becomes a no-start in January — the decline is gradual, but the failure is sudden.
