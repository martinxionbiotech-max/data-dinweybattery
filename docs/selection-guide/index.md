# Truck Battery Selection Guide

**TL;DR** — Choose a truck battery in four steps: (1) confirm the group size, (2) check the
cold cranking amp (CCA) requirement, (3) verify the electrical system voltage (12V or 24V),
and (4) account for climate. Getting the group size and CCA wrong is the #1 cause of fleet
breakdowns.

## Step 1: Confirm the Group Size

The group size is the physical footprint and terminal layout of the battery. It must match
your vehicle's battery tray and cable reach.

| Standard | Common truck group sizes | Market |
|---|---|---|
| JIS | 145G51 (N150), 190H52 (N200) | Japan / Asia / Africa / Middle East |
| DIN / EN | 58827 (DIN88), 60038 (DIN100) | Europe / Middle East |
| BCI | Group 31, 8D, 4D | North America (on request) |

Always read the group size printed on your existing battery, or consult the vehicle manual.

## Step 2: Check the CCA Requirement

Cold cranking amps (CCA) measures a battery's ability to start an engine in cold temperatures.
Diesel engines need more CCA than gasoline engines of the same size.

| Truck type | Typical CCA range (guidance only) |
|---|---|
| Light truck / pickup | 600–800 CCA |
| Medium-duty truck | 800–1000 CCA |
| Heavy-duty / semi | 850–1100+ CCA |

!!! note "Confirm against your manual"
    These are general ranges. The exact CCA requirement is printed in your vehicle manual
    and on the original battery label.

## Step 3: Verify the System Voltage

- **12V** — light trucks and pickups
- **24V** — heavy trucks, semi trailers and buses (two 12V batteries in series)

Ordering the wrong voltage is a costly mistake. See the
[12V vs 24V guide](../12v-vs-24v/index.md).

## Step 4: Account for Climate

Colder climates require higher CCA. In extreme cold, consider AGM batteries, which perform
better at low temperatures than flooded lead-acid.
