# How Many Amps to Jump-Start a Semi Truck

**TL;DR** — A semi truck's starter can draw several hundred amps. Use heavy-duty jumper cables
(2 AWG or thicker) rated for 400–600+ amps and a heavy-duty jump source. Never jump a 24V
system from a 12V source directly.

## The Starting Current

A large diesel engine's starter can draw **several hundred amps** during cranking — far more
than a passenger car. This means:

- **Jumper cables** must be heavy-gauge (2 AWG or thicker) and rated for 400–600+ amps
- **The jump source** must be able to deliver that current — another running heavy vehicle,
  or a heavy-duty jump pack rated for large diesels

## 12V vs 24V Jump-Starting

| System | Jump-start approach |
|---|---|
| 12V truck | Standard 12V jump source, heavy cables |
| 24V truck | 24V source, or two 12V sources in series |

!!! warning "Do not jump 24V from 12V"
    Connecting a 24V system to a 12V source (or vice versa) can damage both electrical
    systems. Match the voltage.

## Safe Jump-Start Procedure

1. **Match voltage** — confirm both systems are the same voltage
2. **Connect positive to positive** — dead battery positive first
3. **Connect negative to a ground** — a clean metal point on the dead vehicle, not the battery
   negative terminal (reduces spark risk)
4. **Start the donor, then the dead vehicle**
5. **Disconnect in reverse order** — ground first, then positives

## Choosing a Jump Pack

For a semi truck, use a **heavy-duty jump pack** rated for large diesel engines. Standard
automotive jump packs are usually insufficient — check the peak amps and diesel rating.
