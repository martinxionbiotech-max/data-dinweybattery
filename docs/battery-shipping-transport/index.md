# Truck Battery Shipping & Transport Rules

**TL;DR** — Lead-acid batteries are classified dangerous goods (UN2794 wet; UN2800
non-spillable for AGM/VRLA). They ship regularly by sea under the IMDG Code with correct
packing and documentation. AGM batteries that meet the non-spillable criteria are the simplest
to transport.

## Dangerous Goods Classification

Truck starting batteries are lead-acid, which the UN system classifies as dangerous goods:

| UN number | Description | Typical battery |
|---|---|---|
| UN2794 | Batteries, wet, filled with acid | Standard flooded SLI |
| UN2800 | Batteries, wet, non-spillable | AGM / VRLA (when tests pass) |

!!! note "Why it matters"
    UN2800 (non-spillable) batteries are simpler and cheaper to ship because they are exempt
    from some of the more onerous wet-battery requirements — one reason AGM is attractive for
    export programs.

## How Truck Batteries Ship

- **Sea freight** — the standard method for bulk battery exports, governed by the IMDG Code
- **Packing** — batteries are secured against short circuits and movement, with terminals
  protected
- **Documentation** — commercial invoice, packing list, MSDS, and a dangerous-goods
  declaration where required

## What to Prepare Before Shipping

1. **Correct UN classification** — confirm whether the battery is UN2794 or UN2800
2. **MSDS** — a current Material Safety Data Sheet for the battery
3. **Packing** — secure against short circuit and movement
4. **Destination rules** — some countries have additional import requirements
