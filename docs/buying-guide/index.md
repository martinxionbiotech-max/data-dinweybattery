# Heavy-Duty Truck Battery Buying Guide

**TL;DR** — Buy a truck battery by matching four things — group size, CCA, voltage and
terminal orientation — then choose the right technology for your duty cycle and buy from a
supplier that provides documentation and warranty.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Group size is non-negotiable** — a JIS N150/N200 won't fit a DIN88/DIN100 tray; the footprint and hold-down decide fit before any electrical rating does.
2. **12V vs 24V is a costly order error** — record the system voltage first; a 24V truck needs two 12V batteries in series, not one larger unit.
3. **Terminal type is the hidden deal-breaker** — A/D post vs T1 conical and left- vs right-positive; the correct footprint with the wrong terminals is still uninstallable.
4. **Capture all 4 specs, not 3** — group size, CCA, voltage and terminal orientation; skipping the 4th is how wrong orders happen.
5. **Documentation beats a low sticker price** — per-batch CCA test data plus a certificate of analysis proves a battery was tested before shipping; a cheap, undocumented battery is a roadside risk (5–10× the battery in downtime) in disguise.

## Step 1: Capture Your Specs

Before buying, record:

1. **Group size** — from the existing battery label or manual (JIS N150/N200, DIN88/DIN100,
   BCI Group 31)
2. **CCA** — the cold-cranking requirement from the manual
3. **Voltage** — 12V or 24V
4. **Terminal type & orientation** — A/D post, T1 conical, left/right positive

The group size is non-negotiable — it determines whether the battery physically fits the tray
and hold-down. CCA is next: too low and the engine will not crank in the cold. For terminal
detail see [terminal types & orientation](../terminal-types-orientation/index.md).

## Step 2: Choose the Technology

| Duty cycle | Recommended technology |
|---|---|
| Standard long-haul | Flooded (economical) |
| Entry start-stop / mid-tier | EFB |
| High vibration / cold climate | AGM |

Technology choice is a cost-versus-durability decision. Flooded is the economical default;
AGM earns its premium in harsh duty. For the full comparison see
[AGM vs flooded](../agm-vs-flooded/index.md) and [what is EFB](../what-is-efb/index.md).

## Step 3: Match the Market Standard

| Your market | Standard | DINWEY availability |
|---|---|---|
| Asia / Africa / Middle East | JIS | Stocked (N150, N200) |
| Europe / Middle East / North Africa | DIN | Stocked (DIN88, DIN100) |
| North America / Latin America | BCI | On request (Group 31/8D) |

The three regional standards are not interchangeable — a JIS battery will not fit a DIN tray
and vice versa. Importers moving stock between regions must match the standard to the
destination market. See [JIS vs DIN vs BCI standards](../jis-vs-din-vs-bci/index.md).

## Step 4: Verify Documentation & Warranty

For fleet, OEM and bulk orders, confirm the supplier provides:

- Per-batch CCA test data
- Certificate of analysis
- MSDS and shipping documentation
- Clear warranty terms

Documentation is where the serious suppliers separate from the rest. For bulk orders,
per-batch test data is the evidence that a battery was actually tested before it shipped —
see [how to read a datasheet](../how-to-read-datasheet/index.md) and
[warranty guide](../warranty-guide/index.md).

## Why Cheapest Is Often Not Cheapest

A battery with inadequate CCA or a thinner plate design fails sooner and can strand a vehicle.
The downtime cost of a breakdown usually dwarfs the price difference. Specify correctly and
buy from a documented supplier.
